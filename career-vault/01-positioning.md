# Positioning

Who you are, what you stand for, what makes you not interchangeable with the next designer a recruiter scrolls past. This is the foundation of everything else in the vault.

Fill this in once during Class 7. Revisit every 3 months. The first version will be rough. That's fine.

---

## One-sentence positioning

*Format: "I am a [role] who [specific verb] [specific kind of work] for [specific market or audience]."*

> Example 1: "I am a UX designer who builds trust-first fintech experiences for first-time mobile users in Bangladesh."
>
> Example 2: "I am a product designer who turns confused PM briefs into shippable mobile flows for BD startups."
>
> Example 3: "I am a designer who works in the gap between offshore client briefs and local market reality."

Try three versions. Pick the one a recruiter would remember 24 hours later.

---

## What I am good at (3 things, specific)

Not "I am good at design." Specific things only.

1. *Example: "Interrogating a brief: finding what is missing before any pixel is drawn."*
2. *Example: "Building for low-end Android. I default to 3G and sub-15K taka devices."*
3. *Example: "Working in the Claude-plus-Figma loop. I am faster at iteration than designers who pick one or the other."*

These should be things you can prove with a project from `projects/`. If you cannot point to evidence, do not claim it yet.

---

## What I am NOT good at (yet)

This sounds counter-intuitive on a positioning doc. It is critical. Junior designers who can name what they cannot do come across as more senior than ones who pretend they can do everything.

1. *Example: "Brand identity from scratch. I am a product designer, not a brand designer."*
2. *Example: "Motion design for marketing. I do functional motion. Hero animations are not my craft."*
3. *Example: "Native iOS or Android. I design for web; I rely on engineering partners to translate to native."*

The act of writing these down also tells you what to learn next.

---

## Where I want to be in 18 months

*One paragraph.*

> Example: "I want to be the senior product designer at a BD-based fintech that hits one million users. The path: ship three more EduBridge-scale projects, build a portfolio that shows the brief-to-launch arc, and be visibly active on LinkedIn so recruiters reach out to me instead of the other way around."

Specific is better than ambitious. "Director at FAANG" is not useful. "Senior at a 50-person BD fintech" is.

---

## My elevator pitch (30 seconds)

When someone asks "what do you do" at a wedding or a meetup, this is what you say. Three sentences max.

> Example: "I'm a product designer. I work on apps for BD users on entry-level Android phones: fintech and edtech mostly. Most of what I do is figuring out the actual problem before any design happens, because briefs rarely have it written down."

Practice saying it aloud. If it does not sound like English you would actually speak, rewrite.

---

## The cheating-objection response (template)

When someone insinuates that AI did your work, you have a calm, prepared response. Write yours now so you are not improvising in an interview.

> Example: "AI accelerates the execution. The decisions are mine. I chose to interrogate the brief before building. I chose to design for parents rather than students. I chose to drop video calling because the budget didn't support it. Those are the calls you're hiring for. The HTML is the easy part."

The full version of this lives in `04-interview-answers.md`. This one-paragraph version is for casual settings (Twitter replies, meetup small talk).
